# Joining the motorway, and traffic joining beside you (issue #8)

Recorded with `node tools/record_evidence.mjs joining` on 2026-10-24: the real application,
stepped at 20 frames a second and captured frame by frame, so each clip plays at true speed unless it says otherwise.
The careful join uses the Bot lab's reference driver (`js/bots.js`); the other clips use the scriptable driver that `npm run test:motorway` drives these lessons with (`tests/mw-driver.mjs`), making the same three safe choices and the one unsafe one the checks make.

## Joining the motorway

Joining from the slip road (Rule 259). The driver builds up speed, looks right in the mirror and over its shoulder, signals, and matches the speed of a gap in the left-hand lane to drop into it. Nobody on the motorway brakes or moves over: traffic already on the motorway has priority.

## Traffic joining keep your lane

Traffic joining beside you, answer one: keep your lane and your speed. The two vehicles on the slip road give way, as Rule 259 says they must, and fit in behind. The lesson passes.

## Traffic joining ease off

Answer two: ease off a little as they arrive, to make room, then back up to speed. Also passes.

[03-traffic-joining-ease-off.mp4](03-traffic-joining-ease-off.mp4) · 42 s · 1.2 MB

## Traffic joining move over

Answer three: once the car alongside in the middle lane has drawn ahead, mirror, shoulder and signal, move over to leave the left-hand lane free, then back left the same way once the joiners are in and two seconds behind. Also passes; being in the middle lane while traffic comes down the slip road is courtesy, not hogging.

## Joining beside a car and what it is marked for

The unsafe join: matching speed with a car in the left-hand lane and running down the slip road into it. Marked as a serious fault (a vehicle alongside), with the result naming it.
